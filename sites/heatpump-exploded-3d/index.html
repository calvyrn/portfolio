Fan & cabinet

A fan that slows down instead of shutting off.

The variable-speed fan runs at the speed the weather needs, so it's quieter than a fridge on most days.

Coil

A big coil pulls heat out of cold air.

Even at −15 °F, outdoor air holds heat. The oversized coil gives the refrigerant more surface to collect it.

Inverter compressor

The compressor is the part that does the work.

It ramps from 30% to 130% instead of slamming on and off, which saves power and keeps rooms even.

Controls & lines

Smart defrost, two thin pipes, no flame.

The board melts frost only when it's there. Two copper lines carry heat into your home. Nothing burns, so there's no carbon monoxide.

Questions Minnesotans ask

Will a heat pump really work in a Minnesota winter?

Cold-climate models keep producing heat well below zero. On the coldest nights output drops, so we size the system for your design temperature and add backup heat where it makes sense.

Should I get rid of my gas furnace?

Not always. If gas is cheap where you are, a hybrid setup often gives the lowest bills. The calculator above shows the comparison for your home.

How loud is the outdoor unit?

Around 55 dB at full speed, about the level of a quiet conversation, and much lower most of the time.

How long does an install take?

One day for a single-zone mini-split, two to three days for a whole-home ducted system.
